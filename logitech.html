Overview

During my three-month UX internship at Logitech G, I contributed to the evolution of the G HUB Account experience, focusing on understanding player needs, improving information architecture, and exploring more intuitive ways for users to interact with their gaming ecosystem.

My work spanned research, concept development, rapid prototyping, and usability testing, in collaboration with the Logitech G team.

This project is protected by NDA. The case study focuses on my role, design approach, and learnings, without disclosing confidential work.

Discovery

Understanding the landscape before defining the problem.

I started by benchmarking gaming platforms and software ecosystems to see how they approach accounts, profiles and personalization. The aim was to build a shared reference of patterns and player expectations before discussing solutions.

Because G HUB serves an international audience, I worked with Logitech colleagues in other regions to understand how expectations vary across markets. Their perspectives shaped the questions I brought into the next phase.

Definition

Turning insights into a clear structure.

I organised a brainstorming session with the team to explore player needs and opportunities, then grouped the findings into themes. I used them to define the information architecture of the Account experience: what players need to find first, and how content should be organised.

Prototyping and validation

Prototyping to explore, testing to learn.

I used Replit to build an interactive prototype quickly, so I could explore flows in a realistic way rather than through static screens. Within a three-month internship, this made it possible to put a working concept in front of users early.

I ran usability sessions with 10 participants, gathered their feedback and used it to refine the experience before the final prototype.
